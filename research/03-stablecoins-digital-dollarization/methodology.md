# Methodology

This research examines whether stablecoins are expanding access to the U.S. dollar and whether growing stablecoin use is beginning to interact with traditional foreign-exchange markets.

## Stablecoin Dollar Exposure

Dollar dominance is based on BIS reporting from 2026, which estimates that roughly 98% of stablecoin value is denominated in U.S. dollars.

Stablecoins are treated as privately issued tokens designed to track the value of a reference currency. They are not treated as equivalent to dollar deposits held in a bank account.

## Nigeria Case Study

Nigeria is used as a case study because of its high stablecoin activity and recent macroeconomic conditions.

Figures are taken from the IMF's 2026 Nigeria Article IV research:

- approximately $59 billion in crypto-asset inflows from July 2023 to June 2024
- stablecoins representing more than 65% of Nigeria's crypto inflows in 2024
- Nigeria accounting for roughly 60% of sub-Saharan African stablecoin inflows from late 2019 to early 2025

These figures describe different measurement periods and are not treated as directly comparable market-share statistics.

## FX Spillover Research

The analysis of stablecoin flows and traditional FX markets is based on Aldasoro, Beltran, and Grinberg, *Stablecoin Inflows and Spillovers to FX Markets* (2026).

The study examines four U.S. dollar-pegged stablecoins across 27 fiat currencies and uses an instrumental-variable approach to estimate causal effects from changes in net stablecoin inflows.

The research reports that a 1% exogenous increase in net stablecoin inflows is associated with approximately:

- 40 basis points of stablecoin-FX parity deviation
- 5 basis points of local-currency depreciation
- 5–10 basis points of higher short-term synthetic dollar funding premiums

These estimates are reported from the published study. The underlying econometric analysis was not independently replicated for this research.

## Dollarization Context

Broader conclusions about digital dollarization are cross-referenced with BIS research comparing stablecoin dollarization with conventional foreign-currency deposit dollarization across more than 130 economies.

Historical dollarization evidence and current stablecoin adoption are treated separately. The research does not assume that current stablecoin adoption automatically produces the same monetary-policy effects observed under conventional dollarization.

## Market Scale

Stablecoin market size and transaction activity are drawn from BIS reporting.

Payment estimates from McKinsey and Artemis Analytics are used separately to distinguish headline blockchain transaction volume from identifiable end-user payment activity.

Because these sources use different definitions and methodologies, their figures are not treated as directly interchangeable.

## Interpretation

The research separates three questions:

1. whether stablecoins increase access to dollar-linked value
2. whether stablecoin flows can spill into traditional FX markets
3. whether those effects are currently large enough to materially alter the broader monetary system

Current evidence supports measurable links between stablecoin and FX markets, but the estimated effects remain small relative to the scale of the global financial system.
