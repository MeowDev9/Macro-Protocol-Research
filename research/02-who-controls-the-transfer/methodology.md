# Methodology

This research examines the difference between blockchain-based transaction infrastructure and the institutions that retain control over transfer permissions, ownership records, and underlying assets.

## Research Scope

The analysis compares four areas:

- Ethereum's public validation layer
- BUIDL transfer controls
- BENJI transfer controls and official ownership records
- USDC issuer-level blocklisting

## Validator Layer

Ethereum validator count was taken from beaconcha.in as a dated snapshot.

Validator count is not treated as equivalent to the number of independent validator operators.

Lido's validator-consolidation figures are treated as projections where applicable, not as completed reductions in Ethereum's validator count.

## Transfer Controls

BUIDL and BENJI were examined as permissioned tokenized financial products.

The analysis focuses on whether a token holder can transfer to an arbitrary wallet or whether the sender and/or recipient must first satisfy eligibility requirements.

USDC is used as a comparison because its issuer can restrict designated addresses through blocklisting rather than requiring every receiving wallet to be pre-approved.

## Recordkeeping and Custody

Blockchain records are treated separately from:

- transfer-agent records
- legal ownership records
- custody of underlying assets

Franklin Templeton documentation and the August 2026 SEC no-action letter are used to identify the transfer agent's role in maintaining the official record of BENJI share ownership.

## Notes

"On-chain" is treated as a description of infrastructure, not as a synonym for decentralization.

Where a source describes a future or estimated outcome, it is identified as a projection rather than an observed fact.
